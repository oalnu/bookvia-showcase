# Product direction and roadmap

Bookvia's intended outcome is straightforward: a provider can publish a useful booking page, receive a booking and payment, and manage the resulting client relationship without a developer's help.

This roadmap describes priorities, not delivery dates or contractual commitments. It summarises the narrower launch direction in the private project rather than reproducing its internal task lists.

## 1. Complete the reliable core

Prioritise the complete provider and client journeys:

- Provider signup → onboarding → service and pricing → availability → published page.
- Client service selection → date/time → details → payment where applicable → confirmation.
- Provider booking/client management and operational notifications.
- Cancellation, rescheduling, refunds and receipt access, including failure and retry cases.

The release gate is demonstrated behaviour in the target environment: consistent policies and pricing, correct booking/payment state, appropriate access control, and recoverable failures. Attractive screens and unit tests alone do not satisfy it.

## 2. Validate a focused pilot

Start with a reachable group of independent providers rather than every service profession. Coaching and personal training are example contexts; the first commercial segment remains a customer-learning decision.

Understand their current workflow, identify a repeated problem, and agree a limited trial. Assess successful bookings, repeat use, willingness to pay and the support needed. Demo accounts, fictional reviews and simulated revenue do not count as pilot results.

## 3. Improve publishing and day-to-day use

Use pilot feedback to simplify page setup, service clarity, booking rules, the calendar and client management. Improve desktop/mobile usability and operational visibility where real use exposes friction. Keep optional features out of the initial navigation when they add complexity without solving the chosen segment's problem.

## 4. Expand only where demand supports it

Candidate extensions include calendar/video integrations, packages, deeper analytics, discoverability tools and session workflows. AI assistance and other growth features remain exploratory. Existing code in these areas does not make them committed launch functionality.

## Commercial direction

A Free/Pro subscription model exists in the application. Final pricing, limits, payment costs and support expectations need to fit the pilot and operating model. Historical pricing experiments are not reproduced as current offers here.
